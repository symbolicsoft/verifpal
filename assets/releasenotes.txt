Since the 0.52.0 release, Verifpal has received two meaningful improvements to the trustworthiness of its analysis, plus some housekeeping.

First, the analysis engine had a subtle bug in how it handled `SPLIT` and `CONCAT`: when a value like `CONCAT(n1, n2)` was split back into its parts, some parts of the engine would always grab the *first* piece instead of the piece actually being referred to, so `n2` could quietly become `n1` inside the engine's reasoning. This caused Verifpal to report an "attack" on a perfectly sound protocol (issue #18). The supposed attacker was just replaying the honest message, but the engine, having garbled its own copy of that message, no longer recognized it as identical.

The fix restructures the rewriting machinery so that every part of the engine now receives exactly the piece it asked for: making this whole class of mistake impossible rather than patching one instance of it, and the engine also learned to recognize that a message which *reduces to* the honest value is still just a replay, not an attack.

Second, thanks to a contribution from Hardik Ghoshal, the parser now refuses to analyze a model that has content after the `queries` block: such content used to be silently ignored, which could hide important model text (like a key leak in a later phase) and make a broken protocol appear safe.

Finally, the codebase was migrated to the Rust 2024 edition and the release packaging (Homebrew, Scoop) was refreshed.
