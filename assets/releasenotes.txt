Verifpal 0.70.0 makes a major change to the language, removing equations completely, and also introduces support for modeling Key Encapsulation Mechanisms (KEMs).

Verifpal has always had a value kind that existed for exactly one purpose. Alongside constants and primitives there were equations, written `G^a` and `G^a^b`, with `G` as a reserved generator, a hard limit of two exponents, and their own rules in the parser, the printer, the equational theory, the unifier and the attacker search. They modelled Diffie-Hellman and nothing else. Diffie-Hellman is now written with two ordinary primitives: `PUBKEY(a)` for a public key and `DH_KEX(gb, a)` for a shared secret, so `ga = G^a` becomes `ga = PUBKEY(a)` and `gab = ga^b` becomes `gab = DH_KEX(ga, b)`. The `^` operator and the constant `G` no longer exist.

Nothing about the security semantics changes. `DH_KEX(PUBKEY(a), b)` and `DH_KEX(PUBKEY(b), a)` are still the same shared secret, and the attacker still cannot compute one from two public keys. What changes is where those facts live: commutativity and the argument restrictions that enforce computational Diffie-Hellman are now declared as fields on a PrimitiveSpec rather than built into the value representation, so the whole equational theory is once again data. Signatures, public-key encryption and ring signatures take `PUBKEY(sk)` where they used to take `G^sk`, and the canonical man-in-the-middle substitution is `PUBKEY(nil)` where it used to be `G^nil`.

Verifpal also gains a key encapsulation mechanism, which is what removing equations was for. `KEM_ENCAP(ek, r)` returns a shared secret and a ciphertext; `KEM_DECAP(dk, ct)` recovers the secret for the holder of the decapsulation key, where `ek = PUBKEY(dk)`. It is deliberately generic rather than named after any one algorithm: ML-KEM, HQC and Classic McEliece are indistinguishable in the symbolic model, and a KEM has no exponent to commute and no generator to sit in base position, which is precisely why it could never have been expressed through the old equation interface.
